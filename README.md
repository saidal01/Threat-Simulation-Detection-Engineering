Summit: Threat Simulation & Detection Engineering (TryHackMe)

This project documents my completion of the TryHackMe Summit challenge, a purple-team focused threat simulation and detection engineering exercise. In this scenario, I worked as a Security Analyst for PicoSecure, collaborating with a simulated penetration tester who executed malware samples on an internal workstation.

The objective was to strengthen the organization's security posture by configuring and tuning security controls to detect, prevent, and respond to increasingly sophisticated malicious activities. Following the principles of the Pyramid of Pain, the project focuses on identifying and blocking indicators of compromise (IOCs) ranging from basic hashes and IP addresses to higher-value behavioral indicators such as tools, techniques, and attacker procedures.

Throughout the challenge, I analyzed malware activity, investigated system artifacts, created detection rules, and improved defensive visibility using a layered approach to threat detection. The goal was to increase the adversary's operational cost, disrupt attack progression, and enhance the organization's ability to identify and respond to future threats.
